## Core Product Capabilities

### Command-Authority Governance

In governed modes, SnapBurst determines whether the active authority is valid for the proposed action, aircraft and operating state.

The presence of a communication link alone does not establish valid authority.

The absence of a link does not automatically remove every previously authorised mission condition.

SnapBurst evaluates the authority that actually remains applicable at the time of the proposed action.

### Authorised-Envelope Enforcement

Autonomous proposals and FreeFlight Recovery actions are evaluated against approved operating conditions before execution.

The authorised envelope may include:

* aircraft identity;
* mission identity;
* permitted task;
* operating area;
* command source;
* current mode;
* aircraft state;
* mission state;
* communications condition;
* and other defined operating constraints.

Proposals outside the authorised envelope are rejected or held before they reach the flight controller.

### Governed FreeFlight

Aircraft operations may continue through simulated C2 interruption without treating missing command authority as active control.

Continuation is limited to actions that remain authorised under the approved mission and operating envelope.

### Controlled Restoration and Handback

Restored communications do not automatically override the aircraft’s current state.

The returning command source must be verified.

The current aircraft state, mission state and active authority must be reconciled before external control is restored.

Authority is returned through a governed transition rather than an uncontrolled switch.

### State Continuity

Aircraft, mission, authority and governance state are retained across command-loss and restoration events.

This allows SnapBurst to evaluate proposed actions against the state that actually exists, rather than treating communications restoration as a return to an earlier or assumed condition.

### Multi-Aircraft Isolation

Each aircraft lane maintains its own:

* identity;
* mission state;
* aircraft state;
* command source;
* authority;
* governance decision;
* dispatch path;
* aircraft response;
* and evidence record.

A condition affecting one aircraft must not improperly alter another aircraft’s authority, state, command path or governance result.

### Decision Evidence

Governance decisions, state transitions, commands and aircraft responses are correlated for:

* testing;
* operational assurance;
* regulatory engagement;
* programme review;
* incident analysis;
* post-operation investigation;
* and future certification evidence.

The evidence record distinguishes between what was proposed, what was authorised, what was dispatched and what the aircraft acknowledged or executed.

## Position Within the Aircraft System

SnapBurst does not replace the aircraft platform.

Existing aircraft and OEM systems continue to provide:

* the airframe;
* propulsion;
* the flight controller;
* communications;
* ground-control interfaces;
* mission planning;
* mission autonomy;
* navigation;
* perception;
* detect-and-avoid capability;
* sensors;
* payload systems;
* and aircraft-specific safety functions.

SnapBurst adds:

* command-authority governance;
* authorised-envelope enforcement;
* C2-loss handling;
* bounded FreeFlight continuation;
* governed command restoration;
* controlled handback;
* aircraft-lane attribution;
* deterministic governance outcomes;
* and correlated decision evidence.

The operating relationship is:

**Sensors detect.
Mission autonomy proposes.
SnapBurst governs.
The flight controller executes permitted actions.
Evidence records what occurred.**

SnapBurst may govern an avoidance action proposed by an external perception or detect-and-avoid system.

It does not claim to detect the obstacle, classify the hazard or calculate the avoidance path itself.

Those functions remain with the relevant perception, autonomy or detect-and-avoid system.

SnapBurst governs whether the resulting proposed aircraft action is authorised to proceed.

## Integration Position

The current implementation and validation environment uses PX4 and ROS 2.

SnapBurst is intended to integrate with existing aircraft, autonomy and fleet systems rather than requiring those systems to be replaced.

A bounded integration would typically connect:

* aircraft identity;
* aircraft state;
* mission identity;
* mission state;
* command-source information;
* authority information;
* mission-command inputs;
* autonomous action proposals;
* communications state;
* flight-controller command interfaces;
* aircraft acknowledgements;
* and aircraft responses.

Within the governed autonomy path, SnapBurst applies governance at the aircraft-command boundary.

It determines whether a proposed action may proceed and creates a correlated evidence record linking the proposal, authority, governance outcome, command dispatch and aircraft response.

Within the current PX4 implementation, the operating relationship is:

**Autonomy proposes. SnapBurst governs. PX4 executes permitted actions.**

The same governance model is intended to be adapted to other aircraft, autonomy and fleet architectures through bounded integration interfaces.
