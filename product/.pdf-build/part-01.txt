# SnapBurst Product Overview

## Governed Autonomy for Unmanned Aircraft

SnapBurst is a governed-autonomy product for unmanned aircraft.

It operates between mission autonomy and aircraft-command execution, controlling which proposed actions are permitted to reach the flight controller.

**Autonomy proposes. SnapBurst governs. The flight controller executes permitted actions.**

SnapBurst is designed for operations in which communications, command authority, aircraft state and mission conditions may change during flight. It ensures that aircraft behaviour remains authorised, bounded, attributable and auditable through those changes.

The product structure is:

* **SnapBurst** is the standalone governed-autonomy product for unmanned-aircraft operators and system integrators.
* **SnapBurstOS** is the licensed integration form through which aircraft manufacturers, autonomy developers and fleet-platform providers can incorporate SnapBurst capability into their own drone products.
* **SnapSpace OS** is the proprietary deterministic governance core beneath both forms.

SnapBurst and SnapBurstOS are specific to unmanned-aircraft applications. Neither provides unrestricted access to the underlying SnapSpace OS technology or cross-industry rights to that governance core.

---

## The Operational Problem

BVLOS and autonomous aircraft cannot assume that command-and-control connectivity will remain continuously available.

When a C2 link degrades or disappears, conventional aircraft systems commonly hold position, return home, land, abort the mission or wait for operator intervention.

Those responses may protect the aircraft, but they also interrupt useful operations.

The deeper problem is not simply loss of telemetry or connectivity. It is loss of clear command authority.

The aircraft system must still determine:

* which authority remains valid;
* what actions remain permitted;
* whether the current task may continue;
* how aircraft and mission state are preserved;
* how restored command is verified;
* how control is returned;
* and how the full event is evidenced afterward.

SnapBurst addresses that governance problem.

It provides a controlled decision layer between proposed aircraft behaviour and physical command execution.

---

## What SnapBurst Does

In FreeFlight Recovery and Autonomous Work Mode, SnapBurst applies a deterministic governance decision before relevant aircraft commands are dispatched.

For each proposed action, it evaluates the available authority, command source, aircraft identity, mission state, operating envelope and supporting evidence.

It then produces one of three controlled aircraft-domain outcomes.

### Permit

The proposed action is authorised under the current conditions and may proceed to execution.

### Reject

The proposed action is outside the authorised conditions and is blocked.

### Hold

The action cannot yet be conclusively permitted or rejected using the available authority and evidence.

It remains contained within the system pending additional evidence, remediation, a second review or human intervention.

Permit, Reject and Hold are SnapBurst’s aircraft-domain governance outcomes. They are implemented through the underlying SnapSpace OS deterministic decision model.

Within the governed command path, SnapBurst preserves the relationship between:

* command origin;
* aircraft identity;
* active authority;
* mission state;
* aircraft state;
* operating conditions;
* governance outcome;
* dispatched action;
* aircraft response;
* and execution evidence.

This creates an attributable record of what was proposed, what was permitted, what was blocked, what was contained and what the aircraft actually did.

## Three Operating Modes

### Manual Flight

In Manual Flight, the human operator commands the aircraft through the approved C2 path.

SnapBurst correlates:

* the command source;
* aircraft identity;
* active authority;
* aircraft and mission state;
* the governance outcome;
* the command dispatched;
* and the resulting aircraft response.

The operator remains the active command authority while the approved command path remains valid.

In Manual Flight, SnapBurst correlates the command source, aircraft identity, authority state, command dispatch and aircraft response for attribution and evidence.

### FreeFlight Recovery

FreeFlight Recovery applies when the approved external command path becomes unavailable.

The aircraft does not move into unrestricted autonomy.

Mission logic may continue proposing actions, but continuation is limited to the pre-authorised operating envelope.

SnapBurst evaluates each proposed action against the remaining authority, aircraft identity, current state, mission conditions and approved boundaries.

Actions inside the authorised envelope may be permitted.

Actions outside the authorised envelope are rejected or held and do not reach the flight controller.

When communications return, external command authority is not restored automatically. SnapBurst verifies the restored command condition, checks the current aircraft and mission state, and manages a governed handback.

**The link disappears. The mission remains governed.**

FreeFlight Recovery is bounded mission continuation during command-path interruption. It is not uncontrolled autonomy and it does not treat unavailable external authority as active control.

### Autonomous Work Mode

Autonomous Work Mode supports approved tasks in which mission logic proposes aircraft actions while the operator retains visibility and intervention capability.

The mission system proposes the work.

SnapBurst governs what may proceed.

The flight controller executes only permitted actions.

Autonomous Work Mode remains bounded by the same authority, identity, aircraft-state, mission-state and evidence controls used across the SnapBurst operating model.

The autonomy system remains responsible for producing mission proposals. SnapBurst remains responsible for governing whether those proposals may reach aircraft-command execution.

## Governed C2-Loss Sequence

A typical SnapBurst operating sequence is:

1. The aircraft begins inside an approved mission and operating envelope.
2. The authorised command source and operating authority are established.
3. Aircraft identity, mission state, aircraft state and authority context are maintained.
4. A C2 interruption is detected.
5. The unavailable external command path is no longer treated as active authority.
6. Mission logic may continue proposing actions inside the approved envelope.
7. SnapBurst evaluates each proposed action.
8. SnapBurst permits, rejects or holds the action.
9. Unauthorised or unresolved commands do not reach the flight controller.
10. Permitted actions are dispatched through the governed command path.
